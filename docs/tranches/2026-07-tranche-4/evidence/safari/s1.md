# Lane s1 — the WebKit cost model, first-party

**Owner verdict:** *"The performance in safari is god awful and nearly entirely unusable."*
**Verdict from the numbers: confirmed, and it is ONE surface.**

- **Rig:** dist build of `65425697` served by `vite preview` on `:4317`, driven by
  `playwright.webkit` (Apple WebKit-2311 — the Safari engine) at **DPR2, 1440×900**.
  No CDP (WebKit has none): every number is an in-page rAF-delta histogram +
  `setTimeout(0)` responsiveness probe, plus macOS `ps` CPU sampled per engine
  process by exec-path with a pre-launch baseline diff (WebKit's real work runs in
  XPC processes reparented to launchd — a browser-pid tree walk misses them).
- **Recipes (rerunnable, beside this file):** `probe-lib.mjs` (the probes),
  `s1-run.mjs <engine> <feel|attribution|all>`, `s1-attr2.mjs` (clean sub-grid
  ranking), `s1-gest.mjs`, `s1-feel2.mjs`, `s1-safari-real.mjs` (real Safari.app
  GPU sample), `smoke.mjs`. Raw output in `s1-*.json`.

## THE ANSWER, up top

The pain is the **grid grain-hoist** — `HandDrawnGrid`'s steady-state boil: four
board-sized `<svg>` siblings (`.boil-frame-layer`), each carrying
`filter="url(#grain-static)"` (feTurbulence fractalNoise + feDisplacementMap),
opacity-swapped on the ~8 Hz beat.

Hiding **only** that surface at settled idle takes the WebKit GPU process from
**624% → 78% CPU** and the page from **4.6 fps → 49.6 fps**. Nothing else on the
page comes close. Chrome is a flat 120 fps / ~0% GPU with the identical markup.

**Dominant mechanism (named):** WebKit does **not** cache a filtered-SVG raster
across an opacity/visibility flip — it re-executes the whole feTurbulence +
feDisplacementMap chain in the **GPU process**, per flip, and the cost scales with
**filtered AREA**. The grid layers are the largest filtered region on the page
(~board-sized, ≈1200×1200 device px at DPR2) flipped 4-deep at 8 Hz, so they alone
burn ~5–6 cores and starve rAF to single digits. The frozen-pose-stack architecture
(W13) is built on Chrome's "raster each sibling once, compositor-swap" model — that
model is **false in WebKit**, and it is most false where the filtered area is
largest.

## (a) BASELINE FEEL — how bad, and where

WebKit, settled idle (rAF p50/p95 in ms; GPU = `com.apple.WebKit.GPU` process CPU%;
WC = WebContent/main-thread CPU%; resp = `setTimeout(0)` realized delay, ms):

| arm | fps | rAF p50 | p95 | max | jank >100ms | >250ms | GPU % | WC % | resp max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| idle unsolved light | **~5** | 206–222 | 226–246 | 257 | 45–47 | 0–1 | **614–644** | ~5 | 14 |
| idle unsolved dark  | **~4.4** | 216–226 | 231–247 | 568 | 42–47 | 0–2 | **611–615** | ~5 | 15 |
| idle solved light   | **3.84** | 256 | 277 | 280 | 31 | **24** | **614** | ~5 | 13 |
| idle solved dark    | — | — | — | — | — | — | — | — | **WebKit process CRASHED** |
| gesture: toggle     | 3.55 | 223 | 589 | 589 | 9 | 4 | **674** | ~5 | 13 |
| gesture: drawer glide | 3.73 | 252 | 678 | 678 | 10 | 6 | **688** | ~9 | 9 |
| gesture: K-peek hold | 50.4 | 17 | 27 | 270 | 2 | 1 | 341 | — | 9 |

**How bad:** at idle the page renders at **4–6 fps** with **every ~200ms frame
janked** — this is the "unusable." The **main thread is fine everywhere** (WC ~5%,
responsiveness max 13–16 ms): input isn't blocked, the *compositor/GPU process* is
saturated (6–7 cores pinned), so the display simply can't produce frames.

**Where:** **BOTH idle and gesture, but idle is the root.** Gestures aren't
independently expensive — they ride on top of a GPU that's already maxed by the idle
grid boil, which is why the toggle and the drawer glide hitch 589–678 ms mid-motion.
The lone bright arm (K-peek, 50 fps) is the tell: holding K glides the board under
the answer-key laminate, which takes the boiling grid layers off the critical raster
path — remove the grid boil and the frame rate returns, exactly as the attribution
below shows. **Solved is worse than unsolved** (3.84 fps, 24 freezes >250 ms; the
solved-dark arm crashed the WebKit process outright — the solver-ink adds filtered
surfaces on top of the grid). The harness crashing WebKit under sustained load is
itself the owner's "nearly entirely unusable," reproduced.

## (b) ATTRIBUTION by surface isolation — the ranked cost table

Idle, unsolved, light. Each surface `display:none`-injected; GPU% and fps re-measured.

| arm | GPU % | fps | Δ GPU vs baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **baseline (all boil on)** | **623.8** | **4.6** | — |
| − toggle stacks | 614.6 | 4.5 | −9 (masked) |
| − logo stack | 594.4 | 4.5 | −29 (masked) |
| − outlines | 575.4 | 3.9 | −48 (masked) |
| − divider | 603.4 | 4.5 | −20 (masked) |
| **− grid grain-hoist** | **78.0** | **49.6** | **−546** |
| ALL boil surfaces off | 34.5 | 60.0 | −589 |
| PRM (beat frozen, nothing hidden) | 20.3 | 60.0 | −604 |

The single-hide deltas for toggle/logo/outlines/divider look tiny **only because the
grid so saturates the GPU that removing anything else still leaves it pinned** (fps
stays ~4–5). Removing the grid is the only single arm that restores frame rate.

**Cost decomposition (GPU-process CPU at idle):**

| mechanism | GPU CPU | share of the boil cost | fps when removed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **grid grain-hoist (`.boil-frame-layer` ×4, board-area feTurbulence)** | **~546%** | **~93%** | 4.6 → 49.6 |
| toggle + logo + outlines + divider, combined | ~44% (78 → 34.5) | ~7% | negligible individually |
| WebKit idle floor (static one-shot filters, wasm worker, compositor) | ~20–35% | — | — |

Clean sub-grid ranking (grid held OFF throughout, GPU un-saturated — `s1-attr2.mjs`):
every remaining surface renders a **flat 60 fps**, and their CPU sits within `ps`
sampling noise of the ~35% idle floor (toggle ~−15%, logo ~−8%, outlines/divider
indistinguishable from noise). **They are not the Safari pain.** The whole problem is
the grid.

**PRM proof:** `prefers-reduced-motion` freezes the shared beat centrally — GPU
drops to 20.3%, 60 fps, with *nothing hidden*. The re-raster is 100% beat-driven; a
static page in WebKit is fine. The filters aren't the problem — **re-rastering them
on every beat flip** is.

Chromium is a **flat 116–120 fps, zero jank, ~0% GPU across every arm** — grid on or
off is *identical*. (A ~10% GPU blip during draw-in settles to 0.) This is the W13
design working exactly as intended: each frozen-filter sibling rasters once, the 8 Hz
opacity flips are pure compositor swaps. **The architecture is correct for Chrome and
pathological for WebKit** — same DOM, 624% vs ~0% idle GPU.

## (d) Real Safari confirmation

safaridriver isn't enabled for WebDriver (`--enable` needs authentication) and
playwright doesn't drive Safari.app — so **playwright-webkit (Apple WebKit-2311) is
the proxy**, the same engine Safari ships. As a no-automation cross-check I opened the
preview in **real Safari 26.4** and sampled the system WebKit GPU process
(`s1-safari-real.mjs`):

| | GPU % | WebContent % |
|---|---|---|
| before opening the tab | 0.4 | 0 |
| **Safari 26.4, settled idle 10s** | **194.5 avg / 217 peak** | 4.5 |

Identical signature: **GPU process saturated, main thread idle.** ~2 cores burned
continuously on a static idle page that should cost 0. (The absolute figure is below
playwright-webkit's ~620% — real Safari's window wasn't pinned to DPR2/1440×900 and
its GPU path differs — but the mechanism is the same and the diagnosis holds.)

## For the fix lanes (what to change, without touching quality)

The mechanism says the cure is **stop re-rastering a filtered board-area layer on the
beat** — not "remove the boil." Quality-preserving directions, in order of leverage:

1. **The grid boil is ~93% of the cost — it is the only surface that must change.**
   Everything else can stay exactly as shipped; it's free in WebKit already.
2. The grid's four `grain-static` layers are the offender **because of filtered
   area**. Options that keep the hand-drawn grain identical: pre-rasterize the four
   grain frames to bitmaps once (image swap, zero filter at steady state — the same
   "raster once" the design *intends*, made real for WebKit by removing the live
   filter node); or bake the displacement into the path geometry (the stroke-boil
   "grain-baked pre-displaced paths" pattern already used elsewhere — no filter at
   all); or, WebKit-only, drop the grid to a single static grain frame (PRM already
   proves a frozen grid is 60 fps and indistinguishable to the compositor).
3. A pencil-boil-level lever: **gate the board-area grain-hoist off on WebKit**
   (engine sniff or a `@supports`/capability probe) and fall back to a
   pre-rastered/static grain — the icon and line boils (tiny area) stay live and cost
   nothing even in WebKit, so the page keeps its motion everywhere it's cheap.

The one-line summary for the loop: **kill the live filter on the four board-sized
grid layers in WebKit; leave every other boil alone.**
