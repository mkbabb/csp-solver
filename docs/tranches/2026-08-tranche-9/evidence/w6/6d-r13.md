## The mechanism

`previewFor` attached the room's clock to the MOUNTED game's still and to nothing else. The
reasoning was sound and the scope was not: peer authorship is a fact about a **board**, not about a
**seat**. The FOLLOW is the counter-example — a peer's switch hands the room's board, peer digits
and all, to the incoming game, which persists it under its own key and then, at the next switch,
becomes a still like every other card. Disk holds digits and never authors, so every one of those
stills drew a peer's 7 in the reading page's own ink, on a card whose live face had always inked
the two hands apart.

The ordering is the whole difficulty, and it is why the cure is two writes and a refusal.
`useSession`'s `st` handler replaces `ledger.clock` with the INCOMING board's **before** it calls
`stageBoardFollow`. Any snapshot taken downstream of that would colour the outgoing game's digits
out of a clock about somebody else's board.

## The cure

A per-id ink bank in the bridge, memory-only, written only where a board and a clock are provably
the same board's:

| write | seam | why it cannot be wrong |
|---|---|---|
| the incoming game | `stageBoardFollow` | one statement after the session adopted that board's clock off the same `st`, synchronously |
| the game in the seat | `bankAuthorInk`, called from App's `watch(authorInk, …)` | REFUSED while a follow is armed, so the outgoing game keeps the snapshot it earned while the clock was still its own |

`previewFor` then reads live for the seat and off the bank for everything else. An empty clock
RETIRES a row rather than banking a blank — which is also the self-healing arm: leaving a room, or
dealing a fresh board over a banked one, empties the clock and the row goes with it, so a still can
never outlive the fact it was drawn from.

**The refusal is what makes the ordering argument hold without a tiebreak.** App runs two watches —
the mounted-id publish and this bank — and neither waits on the other, because during a follow the
bank write is refused whichever ran first, and outside a follow the two facts do not change in the
same tick. The unit row plays both orderings in one test.

## The pi law — a DECLARED delta

This lane moves pixels, and the moved pixels ARE the cure. On a still of a board the table has
turned away from, a peer's digit changes from the reading page's ink to that peer's own.

`r13-frames/card-before-<engine>.png` → `r13-frames/card-after-<engine>.png` (the sudoku card,
element-scoped, both engines): before, the peer's 7 and the reader's 4 are both blue; after, the 7
is the peer's green and the 4 stays blue. The frames were taken with a temporary screenshot line
that was removed before the spec landed — the shipped row reads `stroke` and nothing else.

**No golden moves, and the reason is structural rather than lucky:** the bank is only ever non-empty
when the room's clock is, so on any page with no room `previewFor` returns byte-for-byte what it
returned before. Every golden is solo. No golden was re-baselined and none was touched.

## The residue, stated

The bank is memory-only, so a reload drops it and a still whose disk board carries a previous page
life's peer digits draws them in the reader's ink again. That is not a hole — the session drops its
clock on the same reload, so the LIVE board answers identically. The still and the board agree,
which is the whole of what R13 asks for.
